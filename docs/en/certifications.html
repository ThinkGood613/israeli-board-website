Registered Addiction Trainee (RAT)

Registered Trainee status is for students and new workers who are accumulating education and supervised hours. Registration gives you:

  • a formal supervision agreement with a Board-recognized supervisor
  • an online log of your hours
  • an annual reminder of your progress

Registration is valid for up to 5 years. RAT is a training status, not a certification, and must not be presented to clients as one.

Certified Addiction Counselor I (CAC I)

The entry-level credential, for counselors who deliver individual and group counseling, psychoeducation and case management under clinical supervision.

  • Education: a post-secondary certificate or diploma in addiction counseling, social work, nursing, criminology or a related field from a recognized institution.
  • Addiction-specific training: 270 hours, including 6 hours of professional ethics and 6 hours of cultural competence.
  • Experience: 3,000 supervised hours in addiction services, including a 150-hour practicum across the 12 competency domains.
  • Supervision: at least 1 hour of supervision for every 40 hours of work.
  • Exam: Level 1 written exam.
  • Also required: two professional references and a signed Code of Ethics.

Certified Addiction Counselor II (CAC II)

For counselors who carry a full caseload, carry out assessment and treatment planning, and work with families.

  • Education: a bachelor's degree in social work, psychology, criminology, nursing, education, occupational therapy or another behavioral or health field.
  • Addiction-specific training: 350 hours, including 6 hours of ethics, 6 hours of cultural competence and 6 hours on co-occurring disorders.
  • Experience: 6,000 supervised hours, reduced to 4,000 with a relevant master's degree.
  • Exam: Level 2 written exam.
  • Also required: three references, one of them from a clinical supervisor.

Master Addiction Clinician (MAC)

The advanced clinical credential, for licensed or registered professionals who provide independent addiction treatment, complex assessment and psychotherapy.

  • Education: a master's degree or higher (MSW, MA in clinical psychology or counseling, MSN, MD), together with an active Israeli license or registration in that profession.
  • Addiction-specific training: 450 hours.
  • Experience: 4,000 hours after the master's degree, at least 2,000 of them supervised by a CCS or an equivalent supervisor.
  • Exam: Level 2 written exam plus a case-based oral review in front of two examiners.

Certified Clinical Supervisor (CCS)

For senior clinicians who supervise counselors and trainees, lead teams and train the next generation of professionals.

  • Prerequisite: a current CAC II or MAC credential.
  • Experience: 5 years (10,000 hours) in addiction services.
  • Supervision training: 30 hours covering models, ethics, evaluation and administration.
  • Supervised supervision: 200 hours of providing clinical supervision, with 20 hours of mentoring from a CCS.
  • Exam: clinical supervision exam.

Specialty endorsements

CAC II, MAC and CCS holders can add one or more specialty endorsements. Each endorsement appears next to the credential in the public registry.

GD-E

Gambling Disorder

Covers assessment and treatment of gambling disorder, including sports betting, online gambling and financial counseling for families.

CSB-E

Compulsive Sexual Behavior

Covers ICD-11 compulsive sexual behavior disorder, partner and family impact, and trauma-informed, non-shaming care.

DIG-E

Gaming & Digital Behaviors

Covers gaming disorder, problematic smartphone and social-media use, and work with adolescents, parents and schools.

COD-E

Co-occurring Disorders

Covers integrated care for people with both addictive and mental health disorders, including trauma, PTSD and mood disorders.

Endorsement requirements

  • 60 hours of specialty-specific education
  • 1,000 hours of clinical work in the specialty
  • 20 hours of consultation with a Board-approved specialty consultant
  • a written case presentation
  • 10 hours of specialty-specific CE in every renewal cycle

Physicians: Addiction Medicine Diplomate

Medical specialization in Israel is governed by the Scientific Council of the Israeli Medical Association. The Board does not certify medical specialties. Instead, it recognizes physicians who show advanced competence in addiction medicine with the Addiction Medicine Diplomate designation. You are eligible if you:

  • hold an active Israeli medical license, and
  • meet one of these:
    • you passed the ISAM International Certification in Addiction Medicine
    • you hold ABPM / ABPN addiction certification, FAChAM, or an equivalent recognized by the Board
    • you completed a recognized addiction medicine fellowship and passed the Board's physician exam

Competency domains

All credentials and exams are mapped to 12 core functions, adapted from international addiction counseling standards:

1. Screening2. Intake3. Orientation
4. Assessment5. Treatment planning6. Counseling (individual, group, family)
7. Case management8. Crisis intervention9. Client education
10. Referral11. Documentation12. Consultation & ethics
