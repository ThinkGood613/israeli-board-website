Ethics & Complaints

Every certified professional commits to our Code of Ethics. When concerns arise, we act fairly, confidentially and in the public interest.

Code of Ethics: core principles

1. Client welfare

Put clients' wellbeing and autonomy first, and practice non-judgmental, recovery-oriented care.

2. Confidentiality

Protect client information in line with the Patient's Rights Law and the Privacy Protection Law. Explain the limits of confidentiality clearly.

3. Professional boundaries

No sexual, financial or other exploitative dual relationships with clients or their families.

4. Competence

Practice only within your training and credential. Seek supervision and refer when needed.

5. Non-discrimination

Serve everyone regardless of religion, nationality, ethnicity, gender, sexual orientation, disability or substance use.

6. Integrity

Represent your credentials accurately. No false advertising and no guarantees of a "cure".

7. Personal wellness

Look after your own health and recovery, and step back from practice when you are impaired.

8. Responsibility to the profession

Support colleagues, mentor ethically and cooperate with ICBAP processes.

File a complaint

Anyone can file a complaint about a CASAP holder, including clients, family members, colleagues and employers.

In an emergency call 101 (Magen David Adom) or 100 (Police). For emotional support, call ERAN at 1201. ICBAP cannot handle emergencies.

Your complaint is confidential. The professional receives a copy of the complaint so they can respond. You may ask for your identity to be protected, though this can limit our investigation.

The disciplinary process

  1. Intake

    We confirm the complaint is within our jurisdiction, within 10 business days.

  2. Response

    The professional has 30 days to respond in writing.

  3. Investigation

    The Ethics Committee reviews the evidence and may hold a hearing.

  4. Decision

    A reasoned written decision is issued to both parties.

  5. Appeal

    Either party may appeal to the Appeals Panel within 30 days.

Complaints that may involve a criminal offence, or that fall under a statutory regulator, are referred to the relevant authority.

Possible sanctions

  • Dismissal, or closure with an educational letter
  • Private reprimand
  • Required supervision, training or a health evaluation
  • Public reprimand
  • Suspension of the credential
  • Revocation of the credential, temporary or permanent

Public reprimands, suspensions and revocations are published in the registry and on this page after any appeal is decided.

Duty to report

Certificants must tell ICBAP within 30 days if any of the following happens to them:

  • a criminal conviction
  • a disciplinary action by a licensing authority or employer
  • loss of a professional license

Israel Certification Board of Addiction Professionals

The Israel Certification Board of Addiction Professionals (ICBAP) is a member board of the International Certification & Reciprocity Consortium (IC&RC). It awards the CASAP credential, Israel's equivalent of the IC&RC Alcohol & Drug Counselor (ADC) credential.
