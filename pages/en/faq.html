About ICBAP and IC&RC

What is IC&RC?

The International Certification & Reciprocity Consortium is a non-profit organization that sets international standards and exams for addiction professionals. Its members are more than 75 certification boards worldwide. ICBAP is Israel's member board.

What is the CASAP?

CASAP is the credential ICBAP awards. It is Israel's equivalent of the IC&RC Alcohol & Drug Counselor (ADC) credential and is reciprocal with it.

What does "reciprocal" mean?

Your credential can be transferred between IC&RC member boards. If you move abroad, you can transfer your CASAP to the member board where you settle, without retaking the exam. ADC holders moving to Israel can transfer to ICBAP in the same way.

Is the CASAP required by law to work in addiction treatment in Israel?

No. The CASAP is a voluntary professional credential. Some employers may prefer it, but it does not replace statutory licensing, for example as a social worker, psychologist or physician.

Eligibility and the exam

Do I need a degree?

No. The minimum is a high school diploma or equivalent. A degree reduces the supervised experience you need: 6,000 hours with a high school diploma, 5,000 with an associate degree, 4,000 with a bachelor's and 2,000 with a master's or higher.

What education do I need?

300 hours of education in the ADC performance domains, including 6 hours of professional ethics.

What is the exam?

The IC&RC ADC exam, the same computer-based, multiple-choice exam used by IC&RC member boards worldwide. IC&RC publishes a candidate guide to help you prepare.

For the public

How do I check whether a counselor is certified?

Search the public registry by name or certificate number.

Can ICBAP recommend a counselor or treatment center?

No. ICBAP does not refer clients. To find treatment services, contact the Ministry of Health's addiction services or your health fund (kupat holim).
